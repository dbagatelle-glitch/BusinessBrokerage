Proposed Approach

A 12-Month Apprenticeship in Business Brokerage

A structured plan for learning the business under your guidance, contributing to live deals early, and building toward an independent book of business.

The plan

We start by agreeing on how we'll work together, then move through four phases, each with a clear goal and a checkpoint we can use to decide when I'm ready for more responsibility.

Before Month 1

Align on the working relationship

Agree on terms and expectations in writing before the work begins.

  • Agree on the form of the arrangement (independent contractor or agent agreement under your Transworld office)
  • Commission split, and how it evolves with volume and self-sourced deals
  • How leads and listings are shared during the early phases
  • Who covers training, licensing, marketing, and listing costs
  • License sponsorship under your brokerage
  • Non-compete and non-solicit terms, and ownership of client relationships
  • Expected time commitment and how we'll check in (for example, a weekly pipeline review)
  • Agree on this plan and its checkpoints as the shared roadmap
Checkpoint: A signed agreement and a shared understanding of roles, economics, and expectations.

Months 1–2

Learn the machine

Understand how deals actually get done in your office.

  • Attend Transworld Training - Learn the Transworld systems, listing process, and CRM
  • Complete the NJ real estate salesperson license (75-hour course and exam), sponsored under your brokerage
  • Sit in on seller meetings, buyer calls, and negotiations wherever appropriate
  • Review 10 to 15 closed deal files start to finish: engagement letter, valuation, CIM, LOI, purchase agreement, closing statement
  • Build working knowledge of SBA 7(a) lending
  • Study why recent deals fell apart, not just why they closed
Checkpoint: I can explain the full deal lifecycle and the main ways deals die: valuation gaps, weak financials, failed financing, lease issues, and seller cold feet.
↑ Back to timeline
Months 3–4

Do the work alongside you

Contribute real work product on live deals.

  • Recast seller financials and prepare valuations for your review
  • Draft CIMs and listing teasers
  • Screen buyers, manage NDAs, and run data rooms
  • Lead buyer calls with you listening in
  • Start building a referral network: 15 to 20 meetings with CPAs, estate attorneys, wealth advisors, and SBA lenders in Bergen County
Checkpoint: You trust my valuations and CIMs with light edits.
↑ Back to timeline
Months 5–8

Run deals with a safety net

Serve as primary point of contact on a few engagements.

  • Lead 2 to 3 assigned listings, with you as backstop
  • Run my first seller pitches for listing agreements
  • Negotiate an LOI with your coaching
  • Carry a deal through diligence and closing
  • Begin sourcing my own seller leads
Checkpoint: I've taken a deal from listing through accepted LOI largely on my own.
↑ Back to timeline
Months 9–12

Build my own book

Prove I can generate business, not just service it.

  • Sign listings I sourced myself
  • Maintain a consistent weekly prospecting cadence with business owners
  • Commit to a long-term niche and deal segment
  • Begin a professional credential (IBBA CBI or M&A Source M&AMI)
  • Sit down together to agree on next steps
Checkpoint: A self-sourced pipeline that is growing without your help.
↑ Back to timeline

What success looks like at 12 months

Clear targets so we both know whether this is working.

Strong outcome

  • 1 to 3 closed deals where I played a meaningful role
  • 2 or more listings I sourced myself
  • 10 or more active seller conversations in the pipeline
  • A referral network that sends leads unprompted
  • Able to lead a deal end to end

Acceptable outcome

  • At least one closing I helped drive
  • A first self-sourced listing signed
  • A clear view of my segment and niche

Signs it isn't the right fit

  • Prospecting feels like a grind rather than the job
  • The income ramp looks unworkable even with a growing pipeline
  • No path to the deal types that use my strengths
Closings typically lag effort by 6 to 12 months in this business. A modest first year with a strong pipeline is a better signal than one lucky deal and nothing behind it.
